Ninety minutes, built so every leader leaves having done real work with AI, not just watched it. Each block rests on a principle of how adults learn.

    10 min
  2. A live demo on a task from the roomTheir problem, not a polished example.

  3. How to ask: intent, detail and toneOne pattern that works in any tool.

My AI strategy and 90-day plan

Many organisations adopt AI without a strategy: licences go out, a prompt workshop runs, and little changes. My approach is less about writing prompts and more about embedding AI into everyday work, safely, ethically and effectively, by building AI fluency across the workplace.

From a tool to a collaborator

People work with AI in three ways. A good strategy plans for all three and moves people along at a safe pace, starting with the first.

AI as a tool

Automation

AI carries out a specific task, following your instructions.

For example: turn meeting notes into a list of actions.
AI as a thinking partner

Augmentation

You and AI work together, each bringing what the other can't.

For example: draft a board paper together, then ask AI to challenge it.
AI as a collaborator

Agency

You set AI up to carry out future tasks on your behalf, within limits you define.

For example: a weekly report that gathers its own data, checked by a person before it's shared.

My framework: four skills, four stages

The four Ds are what people learn. The four Cs are how far along they are. Everyone builds the same four skills, and each stage takes them a little deeper. They're skills, not techniques, so they don't go out of date when the tools change.

The four Ds: what people learn

D

Delegation

Thoughtfully deciding what work to do with AI and what to do yourself.

D

Description

Communicating clearly with AI systems.

D

Discernment

Evaluating AI outputs and behaviour with a critical eye.

D

Diligence

Making sure you work with AI responsibly.

The four Cs: how far along they are

Stage 1

Curious

Has heard the hype, hasn't used AI for real work.

Skills focus
Delegation and diligence: which tasks are safe to try, and what never goes into a tool.
How we know
A first real task done within 14 days.
Stage 2

Capable

Uses AI for simple tasks, but not consistently.

Skills focus
Description: a reliable way to ask, every time.
How we know
Weekly use and time saved on routine work.
Stage 3

Confident

Uses AI daily, and knows when not to.

Skills focus
Discernment: checking every output, and building AI into real workflows.
How we know
A workflow in daily use, with checks built in.
Stage 4

Champion

Teaches others and spots new uses.

Skills focus
All four Ds, well enough to teach them.
How we know
Peers trained and new use cases submitted.

The four Ds and the three modes above come from the AI Fluency Framework by Rick Dakan, Joseph Feller and Anthropic, shared under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The four Cs are my own. I'm certified in AI Fluency through Anthropic Academy.

The first 90 days

The plan I'd adapt to any business, phase by phase.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and baseline

Understand how work really gets done, and agree with the sponsor what success looks like.

  1. A listening tour: the sponsor, IT and security, legal, L&D, and at least 15 people doing the work.
  2. Audit access against real use, including unofficial tools.
  3. Run use-case discovery with three teams and score every idea.
  4. Agree guardrails with IT, legal and policy teams in plain language.
  5. Deliver one quick win for a willing team.

You'll haveA discovery readout, a scored backlog, a baseline scorecard and a signed-off plan.

Measured byBaseline captured, three teams engaged, sponsor sign-off.

Days 31 to 60

Pilot and teach

Prove value on a few high-priority workflows, and build the people who will spread it.

  1. Launch two or three pilots, each with a named owner and a success measure.
  2. Build role-based learning: short tutorials plus live workshops.
  3. Recruit and train a champions network through train-the-trainer.
  4. Open weekly office hours and a shared library of prompts and workflows.

You'll haveLive pilots, learning paths for each role, trained champions and a first resource hub.

Measured byActivation in pilot teams, session ratings, champions trained.

Days 61 to 90

Prove and scale

Show the impact in numbers, and make the new way of working the default.

  1. Measure pilots against the baseline and publish short case studies.
  2. Scale the winning workflows to the next teams.
  3. Embed AI in onboarding and in managers' routines.
  4. Set up an intake process and a governance rhythm with your policy teams.
  5. Present a 90-day impact readout and a two-quarter roadmap.

You'll haveAn impact readout, a scale plan, a playbook and a signed-off roadmap for the two academies.

Measured byWeekly active use, hours saved, confidence scores, workflows in daily use.

Beyond 90 days: two academies

The longer-term strategy. The AI Academy takes everyone from Curious to Confident, and the AI Champion Academy develops the Champions who keep it growing.

Curious to Confident

AI Academy

Teaching teams how to use AI in their day-to-day work.

  1. CuriousA live demo on their own task and the rules on what never goes into a tool, so the first try is safe.
  2. CapableRole-based practice in how to ask and how to check, until it's reliable every time.
  3. ConfidentAI built into one real workflow, with checks built in and a 30-day check-in.

Short live sessions, then practice on real work. Part of onboarding for every new starter.

Confident to Champion

AI Champion Academy

Developing AI Champions in each department to train and support their team members.

  1. ConfidentPeople who already use AI daily are nominated by their department, at least one per team.
  2. ChampionTrain-the-trainer on the four Ds, using the same kit as my own sessions.
  3. In practiceChampions run office hours, share what works through the network and spot the next use cases.

A champions network across every department, with a shared library of prompts and workflows.
